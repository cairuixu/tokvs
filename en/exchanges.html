How rebates actually work

A rebate isn't a coupon — it's part of the exchange's official referral program. After you sign up through an invite link, a slice of every trading fee you pay comes back to your rebate account under the official ratio. The money comes from the exchange's marketing budget: you pay exactly the same fees as signing up directly; the only question is whether someone shares a cut with you. Rebates settle on a cycle, and the ratios, cycles and payout forms follow each exchange's official rebate page.

Three reminders: 1. rebate ratios differ by platform and change over time — check the official page before committing; 2. rebates return a share of fees, not principal — they won't save a losing trade; 3. anyone asking for your seed phrase or private keys to "claim rebates" is a scammer.

What the fee gap costs at different volumes

Take $50,000 a month in spot volume (regular tier, taker):

FAQ

First deposit as a beginner — which is the smoothest?

Pick the one with good language support and plenty of P2P merchants. Fee differences barely matter on small capital; what matters is whether buying feels smooth and help is reachable when stuck. Open one, run the full flow, optimize by the fee table six months later.

What does "rebate cap" mean in the table?

It's the maximum rebate ratio in each platform's official referral program. Note "maximum," not "everyone gets this" — your actual ratio depends on volume and current policy. Check the official rebate page for the latest numbers before committing.

Why do some platforms advertise "zero fees"?

"Zero fees" usually waives one line item (e.g. spot maker) while taker, futures and withdrawals still charge — or hides the cost in the spread. Read the full set: maker, taker, futures and withdrawals combined. Judging by one line is how people get fooled.

Will rebates get my account "flagged"?

No. Rebates are part of the official referral program; accounts carry no special mark, and support and risk control treat everyone the same. The only difference is a periodic credit landing in your rebate account.

How accurate is the calculator?

It uses the table's public baseline rates to estimate the "regular user, no rebates" theoretical cost. Real fills depend on VIP tier, rebates and your maker/taker mix — the actual number only comes in lower, never higher. Treat it as an upper bound, not an exact bill.

Are rebates and "fee discounts" the same thing?

Roughly the same idea under different names: both return part of your fees. The difference is mechanics — rebates settle through the referral relationship (auto-attached when you sign up via a referral link), while fee discounts may trigger on conditions like holding the platform token. They can stack; the official site rules.

How do I know if I'm a maker or a taker?

By order type: a limit order that rests on the book is maker; a market order — or a limit order that instantly matches — is taker. Every trade is exactly one of the two.

Why do two people pay different fees on the same exchange?

VIP tiers. Bigger holdings and volume unlock lower fees and negotiable rebates. Our table uses the VIP0 standard tier — the default you get after signing up, and the most relevant one for beginners.

With a small account, does the choice even matter?

Not really. On a few thousand dollars, the yearly fee gap between the four is pocket change — pick the one whose language and support feel right. Once your volume grows, come back and pick by the fee table.

Can I stack a referral link with official promotions?

Usually yes — rebates and official promos are separate systems and don't conflict. But each promo's own terms rule; when in doubt, ask official support.

Are rebate ratios fixed?

No. Ratios are set by each exchange's official referral program and change periodically; some even scale with the invitee's volume. Check the official rebate page for the latest numbers before committing.

Which fees deserve more attention — spot or futures?

Futures. Leverage multiplies the traded notional by several to dozens of times, and the fee gap scales with it. Futures traders should sweat every 0.01%; spot buy-and-hold investors can nearly ignore fees.

Why do withdrawal fees differ so much?

Most of a withdrawal fee is the blockchain network's miner/gas cost, which has little to do with the exchange; the exchange adds a small service margin on top. Even on the same platform, TRC20 vs. ERC20 withdrawals can differ 10x — pick the right network before you withdraw.

When do rebates arrive?

Each exchange's official rebate rules apply — rebates are usually credited to your rebate account on a cycle. See the exchange's official rebate page for details. We never touch your funds.

Do fees change?

Yes. Exchanges adjust fee tiers from time to time; the table shows public baselines compiled in Oct 2026. Always check the official fee schedule before you trade.

How often is this table updated?

We re-check official fee schedules regularly, and each page carries its data date. Spot something off? Tell us through the footer contact.

Does signing up through a referral link really cost nothing extra?

Correct. Rebates come out of the exchange's marketing budget — you pay exactly the same fees as signing up directly on their site. Full disclosure is in the footer.

Can I use Binance, OKX, Bybit or Bitget from my country?

It depends on where you live — see the country availability table above (United States, European Union, Canada, United Kingdom, Australia), checked 30 October 2026. The short version: Binance takes no new users in the EU/EEA or the UK and does not serve the US (use Binance.US); OKX serves the EU under MiCA but cannot offer USDT trading there; Bybit does not serve the US or Canada and runs the EU through a separate Bybit EU entity; Bitget is restricted in the US, Canada and the UK. Always confirm on the exchange's official terms before signing up.

Final advice for beginners

If the table still leaves you torn, three sentences: 1. open first, optimize later — pick any of the big four and run a small amount through the full flow; that's worth more than a week of fee-table research. 2. Fees are saved, not picked — limit orders plus rebates beat platform-hopping. 3. Security always comes first — 2FA, anti-phishing code, withdrawal whitelist, all within 10 minutes of signing up.

One last reminder

This table saves you "certain costs" (fees). What decides whether you make money is "uncertainty" (the market). Don't spend a week agonizing over 0.01% in fees and then lose 10% in a day chasing pumps — get the big things right first; fee optimization is the cherry on top.

Worked example: what a futures trader pays in a year

Say you trade $200,000 a month in futures, all taker orders (regular tier):

  • Binance / OKX (0.05% taker): about $100/month in fees;
  • Bybit (0.055% taker): about $110;
  • Bitget (0.06% taker): about $120.

Over a year, that's a $240 gap between Binance/OKX and Bitget. Stack rebates of 30%–50% and you're looking at $360–600 back annually — real money, worth taking seriously. Of course, this only matters if you actually trade that volume; a few trades a month makes the gap negligible — pick the smoothest platform instead. Plug your own numbers into the calculator above. Figures are public baselines; the official site rules.

Big accounts: fees are negotiable

The table shows VIP0 standard rates. If your monthly volume reaches hundreds of thousands or millions of dollars: 1. VIP tiers kick in automatically, with fees stepping down; 2. you can talk to the official business team about rebate terms — large clients get different deals than the public tiers; 3. market makers (high-frequency liquidity providers) get dedicated maker rebate programs, sometimes even negative fees — the exchange pays you for liquidity. None of this concerns beginners, but know it exists: volume is your only bargaining chip at an exchange.
