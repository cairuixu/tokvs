Binance vs OKX: Which One Is Better?

Both are first-tier; neither crushes the other. The real differences: Binance is the largest by volume and coin selection, with the deepest liquidity. OKX charges a lower spot maker fee (0.08%) and has the stronger Web3 wallet and in-app experience. Pick by what you'll actually do most.

Fees: OKX is slightly cheaper for spot makers

How much is that gap worth? Buy $100,000 of crypto with spot limit orders: Binance charges $100, OKX charges $80 — a $20 difference. Irrelevant for small accounts; meaningful for frequent traders, who should pair OKX with a limit-order habit. Maker vs taker is explained in Crypto Fees Explained.

Scale and coin selection: Binance, clearly

Binance is the world's largest exchange by volume. The deepest liquidity means less slippage on larger orders, and the longest coin list means new and niche tokens usually trade there first. OKX lists fewer coins — everything mainstream is there, and the more conservative listing policy also means fewer junk tokens to step on.

Products: different centers of gravity

  • New-token events: Binance's Launchpool is the longest-running and most crowded; OKX runs Jumpstart with a different rhythm and thresholds. Worthwhile on both sides.
  • Web3 wallet: OKX's built-in wallet is its standout — multi-chain asset management and on-chain entry points are integrated noticeably better. If you'll farm airdrops or use DeFi, it saves real friction. Binance has a wallet product too, a step behind on integration.
  • Copy trading and bots: both offer them; neither is the category leader. That title belongs to Bitget — see the Bitget review.

App experience and support: OKX feels closer

Both apps are fully featured. OKX's interface and in-app support generally feel more responsive, and getting a human answer tends to be faster. Binance's scale cuts both ways: an enormous help-center knowledge base where most answers are findable, but ticket queues that can test your patience.

Security and reserves: same tier

Both publish proof of reserves, both have survived full market cycles and serious stress events. Don't pick between them on safety — your own habits matter more: 2FA on, unique passwords, big balances in your own wallet. See Wallet & Seed Phrase Safety.

Verdict: match yourself

  • Spot only, want every coin → Binance. Liquidity and selection are hard advantages.
  • Trade often, fee-sensitive → OKX. The 0.08% maker fee plus limit orders compounds.
  • Going on-chain, farming airdrops → OKX. The Web3 wallet entry point saves constant hassle.
  • Truly can't decide → Start with Binance. A second account later costs nothing.

FAQ

Can I have accounts on both?

Yes, they don't conflict. Plenty of people keep spot on Binance and on-chain activity in the OKX wallet. Each account needs its own verification and its own security setup.

Which is better for a complete beginner?

OKX's guided flows and support are a touch friendlier on day one; Binance's tutorial ecosystem is so large that any problem is searchable. The gap is small — completing the process once matters more than the choice.

Is a 0.02% fee gap worth switching for?

Do the math on your volume: $100k a year means ~$20 — not worth the move. At $5M a year it's ~$1,000 — worth it. Measure yourself first.

Which has lower withdrawal fees?

Withdrawal fees are driven mostly by the blockchain network, plus a small platform service charge that changes over time. Always trust the number shown on the withdrawal page at that moment, not an old blog post.
