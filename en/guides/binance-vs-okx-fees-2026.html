The short answer

OKX wins spot maker fees (0.08% vs 0.10%); everything else is effectively tied. Spot takers are 0.10% on both, futures are 0.02% maker / 0.05% taker on both, and the remaining differences live in withdrawal networks, fiat rails and promotions — not the headline schedule. For most casual traders, the annual difference is a few dollars. Read on for the line-by-line breakdown, then pick on features.

Deposits and withdrawals

Crypto deposits are free on both — that's industry standard. Withdrawals are where it gets nuanced: both exchanges pass through network costs, so withdrawing BTC or ETH costs roughly the network fee at the time, and the exact number moves with congestion. Neither exchange publishes a fixed "withdrawal fee" you can memorize; both show the live fee on the withdrawal screen before you confirm. The honest comparison: they're structured the same way, and the differences per coin are small and shifting. Fiat rails (bank transfer, SEPA, card) vary more by country than by exchange — check what's available where you live before letting this decide anything.

P2P trading: the "free" that isn't

Both Binance and OKX charge no explicit fee for P2P trades. The cost hides in the quote: sellers set their own prices, typically 0.5–2% above spot depending on payment method and demand. A seller offering bank transfer at 1.5% over spot is charging you 1.5% — there's just no line item called "fee." When comparing P2P, compare the final quote against the spot price, not the fee schedule. Payment options (bank transfer, PayPal-supported flows, local methods) also differ by region on both platforms.

Fee discounts and platform tokens

Both exchanges discount trading fees for users who pay fees in or hold their platform tokens (BNB on Binance, OKB on OKX), and both run volume-based VIP tiers. The exact discount rates change over time, so we won't quote numbers that may be stale by the time you read this — check the official fee pages for current rates. As a rule of thumb: if you're a casual trader, these discounts are rounding errors next to your order-type choice (limit vs market).

Worked example: a $1,000 spot buy

You buy $1,000 of BTC with a limit order (maker) and later sell it the same way — one full round trip:

  • Binance: $1,000 × 0.10% × 2 = $2.00
  • OKX: $1,000 × 0.08% × 2 = $1.60

Difference: $0.40 per $1,000 round-tripped. Scale it to your own volume and you'll see why we keep saying it: for casual traders this decision should be about product, not fees. Do this math with taker orders and the gap disappears entirely.

The 2026 context

Fee schedules are stickier than they look: both exchanges have held these standard tiers for an extended period, competing instead through promotions, fiat-rail coverage and product launches. What does move year to year is everything around the schedule — which coins list first, which countries get full fiat support, and promotional fee rebates. Revisit this comparison annually; the headline numbers likely won't have moved, but the fine print might have.

The fine print: what the tables don't show

Fee schedules are only the advertised part of trading costs. Three hidden ones deserve equal attention. Spreads — the gap between the best buy and sell price — cost you on every market order, and they're tightest where liquidity is deepest (usually Binance on major pairs, though the difference is small). Slippage hits on large orders: your $50,000 market buy won't all fill at the quoted price. And VIP tiers quietly rewrite the schedule above a certain volume — both exchanges discount heavily for high-volume traders, which is irrelevant to beginners but worth knowing exists. None of this changes the verdict above, but it's why experienced traders say "fees" when they mean "total cost of trading." For your first year, the visible schedule plus limit orders covers 95% of what matters.

How to actually pay less on either exchange

Regardless of which you pick, four habits cut your bill more than any venue choice. Use limit orders — maker rates beat taker rates everywhere, and on OKX spot the maker discount is the single biggest edge on this page. Batch your trades — ten small buys cost roughly ten times one big buy in fees; plan entries instead of tapping. Withdraw in batches too — every on-chain withdrawal pays a network fee, so consolidating withdrawals saves real money. And revisit the fee pages quarterly — promotions and tier changes are the only parts of the schedule that move fast, and a two-minute check a few times a year catches them. None of this is exciting. All of it compounds.

Bottom line for 2026

Binance and OKX have converged to near-identical fee schedules because they're competing for the same traders — and that's good for you. The days of one exchange being dramatically cheaper than the other are over at the standard tier. What hasn't converged: product depth, fiat-rail coverage in your country, and interface feel. Choose the exchange you'll actually enjoy using, verify the fees once, then stop thinking about it. The traders who obsess over 0.02% are usually the ones losing 2% to sloppy execution.

Verdict: On fees alone it's nearly a tie — OKX edges spot makers, everything else is level. Open the one whose product fits you (or both; there's no cost to holding two accounts).

Which has lower fees overall, Binance or OKX?

It depends on how you trade. OKX is slightly cheaper for spot makers (0.08% vs 0.10%); takers tie at 0.10% and futures tie at 0.02%/0.05%. For most casual traders the difference is a few dollars a year — pick on features, not this gap.

Are Binance and OKX fees the same in every country?

No. Available products, fiat rails and sometimes fee structures vary by jurisdiction due to local regulation. The numbers here are the standard global tiers — check the fee page for your region on the official site.

Do exchange fees change often?

Periodically, yes. Both exchanges revise tiers, promotions and withdrawal fees over time. Treat any comparison as a snapshot and verify on the official fee pages before committing size.

Is P2P trading really free on both?

There is no explicit P2P trading fee on either platform, but the cost lives in the quote: sellers bake their margin into the price. Compare the P2P quote against the spot price to see what you're really paying.

Which is better for futures, Binance or OKX?

On fees they're tied at 0.02% maker / 0.05% taker. The choice comes down to liquidity depth (Binance is deeper on majors), tooling preferences, and which interface you trade better on — not the fee schedule.

Should I open accounts on both?

Many traders do: it lets you arbitrage fee differences, access each platform's exclusive listings, and keep a backup if one has downtime. There's no cost to holding both accounts.

Where do I check the current official fees?

On each exchange's official fee page (search "Binance fee schedule" or "OKX fee schedule" and use the official domain). Bookmark them — fees are one of the few things worth re-checking quarterly.
