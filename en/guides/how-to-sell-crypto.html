The three ways out: pick your exit before you need it

Every cash-out is one of three moves. Spot selling swaps your coin for USDT inside the exchange — fast, precise, and the default for traders. P2P selling trades your USDT directly with another person for fiat in your bank account or e-wallet. Off-ramp withdrawals convert to fiat inside the exchange and send it to your bank or card where the exchange supports it. Most beginners use spot first, then P2P — and knowing both in advance beats learning either under pressure.

One habit covers all three: never move your full stack in one untested step. Sell a small amount first, confirm the money lands where you expect, then do the rest. The fee for a test trade is trivial; the cost of a wrong address or a bad merchant is not.

Step 1: sell on spot for USDT

On your exchange, move the coin from your Funding wallet to your Spot wallet (free and instant on most exchanges), open the coin/USDT pair — BTC/USDT, ETH/USDT, or whatever you hold — and place a sell order. A market order sells immediately at the best available price; a limit order sells only at your chosen price or better. Market is simpler; limit avoids selling into a sudden dip. For most beginners selling a modest amount, market is fine — just check the price before you confirm.

What it costs: a spot trade pays the taker fee when you sell at market, or the maker fee when your limit order waits and fills later. Standard spot rates on the major exchanges are:

After the sale, your USDT sits in your Spot wallet. From here you can hold it, trade it, or move it to Funding for P2P selling. Nothing about this step touches your bank — that comes next.

Step 2: sell USDT on P2P for fiat

P2P selling mirrors P2P buying. Move USDT to your Funding wallet, open P2P, switch to Sell, pick USDT and your currency, and filter by your payment method — bank transfer, or a local e-wallet where supported. You will see buyers' offers with their price, limits, and completion stats. Pick the same way you would pick a merchant when buying: high completion rate, many completed orders, an old account.

  1. Enter the USDT amount and place the sell order. Your USDT locks in escrow.
  2. The buyer pays you by your chosen method and marks the payment done.
  3. Verify the money is actually in your account before you do anything. Check your bank app or e-wallet balance — not a screenshot the buyer sends you.
  4. Only then tap "Release". Releasing before the money arrives is the single most common P2P selling loss, and it is not recoverable.

Two discipline rules: never accept payment from an account whose name does not match the buyer's verified name, and never communicate or settle outside the platform. "Let's finish this on WhatsApp, faster" is how escrow protection evaporates.

Step 3: withdraw fiat to your bank or card

Where your exchange supports fiat withdrawals, you can convert inside the platform and withdraw directly: USDT to your local currency via the exchange's convert or fiat markets, then withdraw to a linked bank account or card. Availability, rails, and limits vary a lot by country and currency — SEPA bank transfer in Europe, local bank rails elsewhere, card withdrawals in some regions — so check what your exchange offers for your currency before you plan around it.

Expect two kinds of cost here. The exchange may charge a fiat withdrawal fee (flat or percentage, shown before you confirm), and your bank may apply its own receiving charges. Processing ranges from minutes for some rails to several business days for others. As always, the number shown on the confirmation screen is the number that matters — read it before tapping confirm.

How to withdraw from Binance, step by step

Binance is where most beginners ask about cashing out, so here is the standard path. It assumes you already hold crypto on Binance; if your coins are in your own wallet, deposit them to Binance first and wait for confirmations.

  1. Sell to USDT on spot (Step 1 above) if you hold anything else. USDT is the currency P2P buyers want.
  2. Move USDT to Funding: Wallet → Transfer → Spot to Funding, enter the amount.
  3. Open P2P → Sell → USDT, pick your fiat currency and payment method, and choose a buyer by completion rate and order count — not just the top price.
  4. Place the order, wait for payment, verify it in your own bank app, then release the USDT from escrow.
  5. If Binance offers a direct fiat withdrawal channel for your currency, that is the alternative to P2P: convert and withdraw inside the platform instead. Check Wallet → Withdraw → Fiat for what is available to you — offerings differ by region and change over time, so the app is the source of truth.

One Binance-specific note: keep your account's payment methods in your own name. Third-party payments get flagged, frozen, or reversed, and appeals on someone else's payment are a losing battle.

Network withdrawal fees: the cost people forget

Moving coins between platforms or to your own wallet is a different cost from trading fees. Each blockchain charges a network fee that rises and falls with congestion, and the exchange adds its own withdrawal fee on top — shown before you confirm. Withdrawing a small amount on a congested network can cost a painful percentage; waiting for quiet hours, or choosing a cheaper network the receiving side actually supports, can cut it sharply. Never pick a network just because it is cheapest — the receiving wallet or exchange must support that exact network, or the coins may not arrive.

Timing your sale: practical, not predictive

Nobody in this guide will tell you when to sell — price calls are not our business. What is our business is execution: limit orders protect you from selling into a flash dip; splitting a large sale into two or three tranches avoids moving the price against yourself on thin pairs; and selling into strength in stages beats a single panicked market order at the worst moment of the day. Decide your plan when you are calm, write it down, and let the plan — not the red candle — press the button.

Taxes: keep records, get local advice

Not tax advice: crypto tax rules differ by country and change often — capital gains treatment, reporting thresholds, and record-keeping duties are all local. Nothing on this page is tax advice. Keep a record of every sale (date, coin, amount, price, fees, order ID) and check your own country's current rules or talk to a tax professional before filing season, not during it.

The record habit is the whole game. Exchanges let you export trade history, but exports disappear when accounts close and formats change. A simple spreadsheet with one row per sale, updated the same day, is worth more than any end-of-year scramble.

Common cash-out mistakes

  • Releasing P2P crypto before payment clears. Screenshots lie; bank balances do not. Verify first, release second.
  • Withdrawing on the wrong network. The cheapest network is worthless if the destination does not support it.
  • One giant market sell on a thin pair. Slippage eats the "top price" you thought you had. Split it or use a limit.
  • Accepting third-party payments. Name mismatches get trades frozen and appeals denied.
  • Forgetting the fee stack. Spot fee + P2P spread + withdrawal fee + bank charges: add all four before judging a route "cheap".

Selling into strength: a worked example

Imagine you hold 0.5 BTC bought at various prices and want to exit half. The sloppy version is one market sell of 0.25 BTC during a sharp dip — you pay the taker fee and eat whatever slippage the thin order book hands you. The disciplined version: set two limit sells, 0.125 BTC each, at prices you chose while calm, and let them fill as the market comes to you. You pay maker fees instead of taker fees where the orders rest, you avoid the worst minute of the day, and you still exit the same total. The difference is rarely dramatic on one trade — but repeated across dozens of exits, it is the gap between a plan and a reaction.

How do I sell crypto on Binance?

Sell your coin for USDT on the spot market, move the USDT to your Funding wallet, then sell it on P2P to a verified buyer — or use Binance's fiat withdrawal channel if one is available for your currency.

What fees do I pay when selling crypto?

A spot trade pays the maker or taker fee (Binance 0.10%/0.10%, OKX 0.08%/0.10%, Bybit 0.10%/0.10% spot — confirm on the official fee page). P2P selling usually has no platform commission; the cost is the buyer's spread. Withdrawals add network and possibly bank fees.

How long does it take to cash out?

Spot selling is instant. P2P selling takes minutes to a few hours depending on the buyer. Bank withdrawals range from minutes to several business days depending on the rail and region.

Is P2P selling safe?

Yes when you follow the rules: escrow holds your USDT, you verify payment in your own bank app before releasing, and you never settle outside the platform. Releasing before payment arrives is the main way people lose money.

Do I pay tax when I sell crypto?

That depends on your country — rules, rates, and reporting duties vary and change. This page is not tax advice: keep records of every sale and check your local rules or consult a tax professional.

Can I withdraw crypto directly to my bank account?

Banks do not receive crypto directly. You sell for fiat first — via P2P or the exchange's fiat withdrawal channel — and the fiat goes to your bank. Any service promising a direct crypto-to-bank transfer is worth double-checking.
