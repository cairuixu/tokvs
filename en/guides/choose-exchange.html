How to Choose an Exchange: 6 Checks That Matter

The short answer: beginners should only pick from the global top tier. Binance, OKX, Bybit and Bitget are the four mainstream choices — similar on safety, different on fees, product focus and app feel. These six checks let you judge any platform yourself.

Check 1: Size and proof of reserves

Big platforms have both less motive and less room to disappear with deposits. More important is whether they publish proof of reserves (PoR) — evidence that user assets exist 1:1 on-chain rather than being quietly lent out. All four majors publish reserve pages you can inspect. No reserves page, no signup — treat it as a baseline filter.

Check 2: Track record

Platforms that survived full bull-bear cycles and extreme volatility days without freezing withdrawals have earned some trust. Also search their incident history: a past hack isn't disqualifying — how they handled it is. Full reimbursement and a public post-mortem is what good looks like; silence and cover-ups mean walk away.

Check 3: Transparent fees

Trustworthy platforms publish their full fee schedule: spot maker/taker, futures maker/taker. The going rate is ~0.1% spot and ~0.05% futures taker. Much higher than that deserves suspicion; hidden entirely means exclude. Compare the four side by side on our comparison page, and learn the math in Crypto Fees Explained.

Check 4: Working fiat on-ramps

For a beginner, the daily experience hinges on funding: can you buy USDT quickly, and do withdrawals actually arrive? Deep P2P merchant benches and reliable rails are a big-four advantage. Obscure platforms are where "easy to deposit, impossible to withdraw" happens.

Check 5: Security features

  • Two-factor authentication, anti-phishing codes, withdrawal address whitelists
  • Most assets in cold storage, plus a risk/insurance fund
  • Login alerts and device management

All four majors have these — the gap is whether you turn them on. Do it the day you register; the checklist is in Step 3 of the Beginner Path.

Check 6: Support you can actually reach

When something goes wrong, reachable support matters more than a pretty interface. In our experience OKX and Bitget respond relatively well in-app; Binance's sheer size means ticket queues can be slower, with more self-service through the help center.

FAQ

Can an exchange still run off with my money?

It has happened historically, which is exactly why you stick to the top tier — and add your own layer: don't keep large long-term holdings on any single platform. Move them to your own wallet; see Wallet & Seed Phrase Safety.

Do I need accounts on several exchanges?

One is enough for a beginner. Every extra account is extra security surface and bookkeeping. Open a second only when you have a concrete reason, like an exclusive campaign.

Is signing up through a referral link risky?

Referral programs are run by the exchanges themselves, and signing up through one never raises your price — it can lower your effective fees. The one thing to verify is the domain: the signup page must sit on the exchange's real domain (okx.com, binance.com). Anything else, close it.

Which exchange has the lowest fees?

For regular users they're close: OKX's spot maker at 0.08% is the lowest of the four, most others are 0.10%; futures taker is 0.05% at Binance and OKX, 0.055% at Bybit, 0.06% at Bitget. Full table on the comparison page.
