How to Choose an Exchange: 6 Checks That Matter

The short answer: beginners should only pick from the global top tier. Binance, OKX, Bybit and Bitget are the four mainstream choices — similar on safety, different on fees, product focus and app feel. These six checks let you judge any platform yourself.

Check 1: Size and proof of reserves

Big platforms have both less motive and less room to disappear with deposits. More important is whether they publish proof of reserves (PoR) — evidence that user assets exist 1:1 on-chain rather than being quietly lent out. All four majors publish reserve pages you can inspect. No reserves page, no signup — treat it as a baseline filter. Binance, for example, refreshes its reserve snapshots on its official PoR page on a regular basis; always verify on the official domain, never trust screenshots or third-party reposts.

All four have survived at least one full bull-bear cycle (including the 2021–2022 drawdown). That is the fundamental difference between them and no-name platforms: extreme markets are the best stress test there is.

Check 2: Track record

Platforms that survived full bull-bear cycles and extreme volatility days without freezing withdrawals have earned some trust. Also search their incident history: a past hack isn't disqualifying — how they handled it is. Full reimbursement and a public post-mortem is what good looks like; silence and cover-ups mean walk away.

Regular-user baselines: spot mostly 0.10%, OKX maker 0.08%; futures taker 0.05%–0.06%. Fee schedules can change — check the official site.

Check 3: Transparent fees

Trustworthy platforms publish their full fee schedule: spot maker/taker, futures maker/taker. The going rate is ~0.1% spot and ~0.05% futures taker. Much higher than that deserves suspicion; hidden entirely means exclude. Compare the four side by side on our comparison page, and learn the math in Crypto Fees Explained.

Merchant count and uptime are the hard metrics: before placing a P2P order, glance at the merchant's 30-day trade count and rating. That tells you more than any ad.

Check 4: Working fiat on-ramps

For a beginner, the daily experience hinges on funding: can you buy USDT quickly, and do withdrawals actually arrive? Deep P2P merchant benches and reliable rails are a big-four advantage. Obscure platforms are where "easy to deposit, impossible to withdraw" happens.

Take Binance's SAFU fund as an example — top platforms typically run user-protection funds, but the payout terms differ. Spend two minutes on the official explanation before you sign up.

Check 5: Security features

  • Two-factor authentication, anti-phishing codes, withdrawal address whitelists
  • Most assets in cold storage, plus a risk/insurance fund
  • Login alerts and device management

All four majors have these — the gap is whether you turn them on. Do it the day you register; the checklist is in Step 3 of the Beginner Path.

For urgent issues use live chat, not email. Chinese-language support hours vary by platform — confirm someone is actually on shift before you trade late at night.

Check 6: Support you can actually reach

When something goes wrong, reachable support matters more than a pretty interface. In our experience OKX and Bitget respond relatively well in-app; Binance's sheer size means ticket queues can be slower, with more self-service through the help center.

Fee math: what the difference actually costs

0.10% vs. 0.08% looks like a rounding error — until you multiply it by volume. Say you trade $10,000 of spot a month:

  • At 0.10%: about $10 in fees;
  • At 0.08% (OKX maker tier): about $8;
  • Stack rebates on top and the yearly gap can cover a nice dinner out — for active traders, multiply that by ten.

The takeaway: the more you trade, the more it pays to pick a low-fee, high-rebate platform; if you barely trade, pick the one that feels smoothest. Figures are regular-tier baselines; check the official site.

Pre-signup checklist

  1. Check the domain, character by character — enter from the address bar, a button on this site, or your own bookmark. Never from a link forwarded in a group chat.
  2. Where the app comes from — official site or your phone's app store only; verify the developer name after installing.
  3. 2FA and anti-phishing code — enable everything within 10 minutes of registering. Cheapest security investment you'll ever make.
  4. Small test first — run your first deposit and withdrawal small, confirm speed and flow, then scale up.

When you actually need a second exchange

One is enough for beginners. Consider a second when: 1. a coin you want isn't listed on your first; 2. a promotion's rewards outweigh the signup hassle; 3. you start trading futures and want to compare book depth and fees. Before opening number two, get security right on number one (2FA, withdrawal whitelist) — more accounts mean more to manage.

Moving exchanges: a migration checklist

Don't just "withdraw and walk." In order: 1. finish sign-up, verification and 2FA on the new platform; 2. test with a small withdrawal and confirm timing and network; 3. move large amounts in batches — if you pick the wrong network, the damage stays contained; 4. revoke API keys and authorizations on the old platform; 5. keep the old account (don't delete it — you may need the records) or close it via the official process. Keep login alerts on at both places during the move.

Testing support: how to tell if it's actually good

Don't trust the "24/7 support" badge. Test it yourself in three minutes:

  1. Open live chat and ask a real question (e.g. "how long does a P2P appeal usually take?"). Judge response speed and answer quality — a bot that dodges the question won't save you in a real emergency.
  2. Check whether help docs are genuinely localized or machine-translated. Machine-translated docs can actively mislead you at the worst moment.
  3. Look at official community activity (Telegram, Discord). When something breaks, community mods often respond faster than tickets.

Remember: support is your only lifeline when things go wrong. Three minutes of testing before signup beats three days waiting on a ticket after.

FAQ

Can an exchange still run off with my money?

It has happened historically, which is exactly why you stick to the top tier — and add your own layer: don't keep large long-term holdings on any single platform. Move them to your own wallet; see Wallet & Seed Phrase Safety.

Do I need accounts on several exchanges?

One is enough for a beginner. Every extra account is extra security surface and bookkeeping. Open a second only when you have a concrete reason, like an exclusive campaign.

Is signing up through a referral link risky?

Referral programs are run by the exchanges themselves, and signing up through one never raises your price — it can lower your effective fees. The one thing to verify is the domain: the signup page must sit on the exchange's real domain (okx.com, binance.com). Anything else, close it.

Which exchange has the lowest fees?

For regular users they're close: OKX's spot maker at 0.08% is the lowest of the four, most others are 0.10%; futures taker is 0.05% at Binance and OKX, 0.055% at Bybit, 0.06% at Bitget. Full table on the comparison page.

Is an account opened via referral link any different?

No. Same features, same fee tiers, same support. The only difference: a slice of your trading fees comes back to you under the exchange's official referral program — same service, plus cashback. Terms follow each exchange's official referral page.

App vs. website — any difference?

Functionally the same; the app adds mobile conveniences like quick-buy and QR flows. Beginners should install both: the app for daily use, the website for carefully reviewing large orders.

Can I trust free exchange rankings?

Check who pays for the ranking. Many "top 10 exchange" lists are ad inventory — number one paid for the spot. The test: does it publish its methodology and disclose ad relationships? Our rule is simple: only official public data (fee schedules, reserve pages), never marketing copy — and our methodology is stated at the top of this page.

What documents do I need to sign up?

Three things: a valid government ID (passport or national ID), a phone number or email you control, and a phone that can run the app and receive codes. Some platforms add a face check — do it in good lighting and you'll pass first try. Submit documents only inside the official app; any "support agent" DMing you for ID photos is a scammer.

Can I open multiple accounts on one exchange?

Legitimate platforms allow one verified primary account per person — duplicates get flagged. If you want separation (say, long-term holds vs. active trading), use sub-accounts: all four majors support them, with free internal transfers.
