What Bitcoin is

Bitcoin (BTC) is a form of digital money born in 2009. Think of it as money written into a global ledger: every transfer is recorded in a public book (the blockchain) that anyone can inspect but nobody can rewrite. No company or government issues it, and no institution can freeze your Bitcoin or quietly print more of it.

The biggest difference from a bank balance: a bank balance is essentially an IOU from your bank, while Bitcoin is an asset you actually hold and control — as long as it sits in a wallet you control. Step 5 covers that part.

Why it has value

  • A hard cap. There will only ever be 21 million Bitcoin. The rule is written into the code and no one can change it. Roughly every four years, new issuance halves — it keeps getting scarcer.
  • Globally reachable. With an internet connection you can send it anywhere, across borders, outside banking hours, arriving in minutes.
  • Running for over a decade. Since 2009 the network has never gone down and never been successfully hacked — that track record is itself a source of trust.
  • Consensus. Ultimately, it's valuable because enough people worldwide agree it is — much like gold. Since US spot ETFs were approved, institutional money has been buying in steadily (the ETF flows on our homepage track exactly that).

Three misconceptions almost every beginner has

1. "One Bitcoin costs so much — I can't afford it"

Bitcoin divides down to one hundred-millionth of a coin. You can start with $20 worth, the same way you can buy 1 gram of gold instead of a kilogram.

2. "Crypto is all a scam"

The technology itself is neutral. The real scams are fake exchanges, "signal gurus," and projects promising guaranteed returns — anything promising risk-free profit is after your principal, 99% of the time. Stick to top global exchanges and never hand money to someone to "invest for you," and you'll dodge most traps.

3. "I'll wait for the exact bottom and go all in"

Nobody can call bottoms reliably. Bitcoin routinely swings 5%–10% in a day and has halved in value multiple times in its history. The sturdier beginner approach: only money you can afford to lose, small amounts, bought in stages.

Risk, stated plainly: crypto is far more volatile than stocks or funds — a 20%–30% drop in a short window is entirely possible. Never borrow to buy, never use living expenses, never use leverage. Those three rules are how beginners stay alive.

All you need to remember from this page

  • Bitcoin = a digital asset with a fixed 21M supply and global consensus
  • You can buy a fraction — $20 is enough to start
  • Idle money only, top platforms only, ignore "guaranteed profit"
